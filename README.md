## 🖥️ How It Works (Plain English)

Think of jev-reviewer as a very smart search engine for a single PDF file.

1. **Upload Your PDF** – Drag and drop a trial report or its supplementary file into the browser window. The file stays on your computer; nothing is uploaded to a server.
2. **Pick a Template or Write Your Question** – Choose RoB 2 for randomized trials, ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies, QUADAS-2 for diagnostic accuracy, or TIDieR for intervention descriptions. Or just type your own question.
3. **Jev Reads and Finds** – The tool scans the entire document and pulls out every sentence that could answer your question. It shows you the exact quote, the page number, and a highlighted view of the original text.
4. **You Check and Approve** – Read the quote in context. If it is accurate, click "Approve." If not, rephrase your question or move on.
5. **Export Your Table** – When all your questions are answered, export the results as a spreadsheet or a table you can paste into your review document.

Let us walk through a realistic example. You are reviewing a randomized controlled trial for a Cochrane review.

1. You download the trial PDF and open jev-reviewer.

5. It shows you three candidate quotes. The first one, on page 4, says: *"Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either drug X or placebo using a computer-generated random sequence."*
6. You see the quote highlighted in the original PDF. You confirm it answers the question and click "Approve."
7. You move to the next question. The tool finds text about blinding. You approve a quote from page 5.
8. After working through all five domains of RoB 2, you click "Export."

## 🧩 Templates You Can Trust

The included templates are not generic. They follow the official published frameworks:

- **RoB 2** – The revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials. Covers randomization, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, and selection of the reported result.
- **ROBINS-I** – For non-randomized studies of interventions. Covers confounding, selection bias, classification of interventions, deviations, missing data, measurement of outcomes, and selection of reported results.
- **QUADAS-2** – For diagnostic accuracy studies. Covers patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow and timing.
- **TIDieR** – For intervention description and replication. Covers the 12 items needed to fully describe an intervention.

Each template breaks down into individual questions. You do not have to answer them all if some are not relevant. You can also add your own custom questions at any time.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to install anything else?**
No. The application runs entirely in your browser. You do not need Python, Java, or any other software.

**Can I use PDFs with scanned images?**
Yes, if the PDF contains text that is selectable or searchable. If your PDF is a pure image scan, you will need to run OCR (optical character recognition) first. Many free tools like Adobe Acrobat Online can do this.

**Is there a limit on file size?**
Very large PDFs (over 100 MB) may slow down your browser. For typical trial reports (2–10 MB), performance is smooth.

**What happens if I close my browser?**
Your session data is lost. Make sure to export your table before closing. You can upload the PDF again and re-ask your questions if needed.

**Can I use this for non-medical literature?**
Absolutely. Any PDF-based review that requires verbatim quotes with page numbers will benefit from jev-reviewer. It is just that the built-in templates are tailored to health evidence.

## 🧠 Tips for Best Results

- **Ask precise questions** – Instead of "What was the outcome?", ask "What was the primary outcome and how was it measured?"
- **Use template questions verbatim** – The templates are already worded to match the tools' guidance.
- **Check page numbers** – jev-reviewer gives you the page as printed in the PDF. This may differ from the journal's printed page numbers. Use the PDF page for your records.
- **Approve quotes in context** – Always click to see the highlight in the original text. A quote can look correct on its own but be misleading if the surrounding sentences are different.
- **Export often** – If you are working on a long paper, export halfway through so you have a backup.

## 🧰 Troubleshooting

**The app does not open.**
Make sure you are using a supported browser. Try a different browser if the first one fails.

**The PDF will not upload.**
Check that the file is not corrupted. Try opening it in a regular PDF reader first.

**The tool cannot find an answer.**
Rephrase your question. Use synonyms from the paper. For example, if the paper says "adverse events" and you ask "side effects," the tool might not connect them. Try asking with the exact words used in the paper.

**The quote is too long or too short.**
You can click on any quote and select a smaller or larger portion of the text directly from the PDF view. Then approve the modified quote.
